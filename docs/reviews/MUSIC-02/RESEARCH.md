# MUSIC.02 — Rewrite research and composition decisions

Accessed 2026-10-07. The user rejected MUSIC.01 as too similar and excessively pitch-bent, and explicitly requested thirty fresh, minimal, individual pieces in the broad Minecraft / Pokémon / Terraria game-music tradition. Changing the old files' keys or tempos would not satisfy that request. None of the prior catalogue or composition functions is imported by this edition.

## What the composers actually said

- **C418, first-person interview, 21 August 2015:** he describes ambient influences, admiration for music that works without percussion, the loneliness of early Minecraft, and the importance of pauses between music appearances. He also describes music that leaves room for the player's experience instead of always announcing the current action. [Red Bull Music Academy interview](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2015/08/c418-interview/). **Our decision:** tracks 11, 20 and 29 use exposed notes, long rests and slow harmonic movement; most pieces have no percussion. This is an original compositional response, not a claim that these pieces reproduce C418's methods or melodies.
- **Scott Lloyd Shelly, first-person interview, 13 November 2013:** he describes combining an affectionate 8-bit reference with larger sounds, composing from biome imagery, keeping arrangements sparse outside boss music, and moving between sections before repeated listening becomes tiresome. [Cheerful Ghost interview](https://cheerfulghost.com/jdodson/posts/1733/interview-with-terraria-composer-scott-lloyd-shelly). **Our decision:** little pulse/mallet cues coexist with soft keys and broader battle colors. The danger and battle cues have distinct meters, gestures and middle sections; the ambient cues are not slowed battle arrangements.
- **Junichi Masuda, first-person official interview, 13 May 2014, preserved by a third-party archive:** he stresses setting a scene, thinking about player experience, studying different kinds of music, and the variety of genres within Pokémon's world. [Archived Pokémon.com interview](https://www.pocketmonsters.net/content/Interview_A_Conversation_with_Pokemons_Musical_Maestro). The provenance is an archived official interview, not a current official page. **Our decision:** each Critz scene gets a specific musical premise. The friendship cue is literally a two-voice exchange; the home cue is a waltz; the workshop cue groups seven eighths as 2+2+3. No Pokémon tune, sound bank or recording is used.

## Theory put into the scores

[Open Music Theory's treatment of sentences](https://openmusictheory.github.io/sentence.html) explains how a basic idea can return, then fragment and acquire momentum toward a cadence. Repetition inside one piece helps it become intelligible; it is not a justification for reusing the same piece thirty times. These scores use different phrase lengths and forms: a two-person dialogue (04), an ostinato with changing melody (16), short 3/8 cells (22), an ambient constellation (20), a rondo-like battle (15), and a through-composed epilogue (30).

The following are **our score decisions**, not measured claims about the reference soundtracks:

- Every score uses absolute pitches and locally authored rhythms, voicings, entries and section order. There is no global A/B/bridge/coda generator, cross-track transposition or shared melody import. Reprises and local repeated bass/comping figures are intentional within individual songs.
- Thirty different quarter-note BPM values span 52–176. Compound-meter BPM is still quarter-note BPM in MIDI; do not mislabel it as dotted-quarter tempo. Meters are 2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 5/4, 3/8, 5/8, 6/8 and 7/8.
- Open bass spacing keeps low chords from becoming muddy. Explicit upper voicings move between common tones and close neighboring notes. Some pieces have no chord pad at all. Some use root/fifth support; the lo-fi cue uses sevenths/ninths. The scare cue's E–F friction and modal color are intentional notes, not detuned playback.
- Major/minor and modal color serve particular stories. The Dorian sixth in 19 suggests gradual recovery; the raised fourth in 07/20 supports curiosity/wonder; 23 visits the relative minor; 16 ends with a major third as a deliberate final lift. 29 restricts its melody to D-major pentatonic notes.
- Melodies leave breathing space. Answers sometimes occupy silence rather than doubling the lead. Changes in density are structural, not a permanent seven-part backing under every tune.
- **Zero nonzero pitch-wheel events.** Portamento, modulation and chorus controls are zero. Oscillators have fixed A440 equal-tempered fundamentals. There is no detuned unison, pitch LFO, tape wow or note scoop. Drums may use a percussive kick frequency sweep; that is not a pitched melody glide.
- Actual sine, triangle, square, pulse and saw synthesis uses different harmonic spectra. The source also defines piano, electric piano, mallet, bell, nylon, reed, flute and other original patches. Band-limited harmonic sums and shaped envelopes avoid hard digital edges. The spectral tests distinguish the core waveforms and check all patches' A4 fundamentals.

## What MIDI does and does not preserve

The delivered Type-1 MIDI files are the source of the listening-room performances: named parts, absolute pitches, timing, velocity, volume, pan, tempo and section markers. The renderer reparses the saved MIDI. Standard MIDI files do **not** contain arbitrary oscillator waveforms. GM program changes are portable fallback choices, not a promise that another sound bank will sound identical. Recreate the previews with the included synthesis source, or assign your own instruments in a DAW. These are complete short pieces, not tested seamless gameplay loops.

## Verification and limits

`validation.json` checks MIDI note lifecycles, no overlapping identical notes on a channel, tempo/meter/marker integrity, absence of pitch glides, archive identity, unique six-note interval/onset fingerprints, comparison against the rejected edition, waveform spectra, MIDI-derived rendering, decoded durations, loudness, true peaks and stereo compatibility. The fingerprint comparison removes absolute key and tempo, so simple transpositions or playback-speed changes cannot masquerade as new tunes. It is a limited similarity diagnostic, not a musicological proof of uniqueness. Intentional quiet passages are counted, not rejected as errors.

The browser report checks every audio file starts and seeks, downloads, filters, queue behavior, mobile layout and unchanged synthetic game storage. Technical checks do not establish beauty or replace human listening. No physical iPhone/Safari or human headphone listening is claimed. This edition awaits the user's response and remains outside gameplay.
