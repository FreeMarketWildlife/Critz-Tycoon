What are we comparing, and what did Emerald do?

A–D change the visible material split only. All three fixtures use native pixels translated inside a padded assembly, shifted one full tile south relative to the fixed collision mask. The collision cells and Hero are identical. B places the base at the half mark; D reproduces the current four-pixel ground band. Percentages are measured from the tile’s top and describe the front/base edge.

Movement matches the main game. Every step commits to one full tile. The same controller supplies turns, alternating walk poses and blocked-wall behavior. All options use the same tile anchors and collision, so their spacing can be compared fairly. A larger ground strip increases the visible gap; collision is never inferred from the art.

Emerald does not use one universal fraction. The measured house and indoor north-wall examples fill the whole reference tile. A sampled ledge is about half grass / half rock. Sampled cliff feet are mostly grass and walkable; our structural transition cells deliberately remain blocked as requested. Exact pinned measurements and limitations ↗

Words we’ll use: transition tile = a material and/or collision boundary within one cell; cliff = mountain edge/wall; ledge = a short one-way jump. Roof projection can hide Hero behind a house while the feet remain on clear ground. The test house is closed so its front boundary can be compared without a door warp.
